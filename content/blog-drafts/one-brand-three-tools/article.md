# One Brand, Three Tools: Set Up Your System and Make a Campaign

AI can make twenty versions of an ad before you've decided what the brand should sound like. [Higgsfield's new Ads Studio](https://higgsfield.ai/blog/higgsfield-ads-studio), for example, can generate up to twenty **static** ads for a product in one run from saved brand and product information. That speed is useful when you need variations. It also makes an old problem more visible: if your brand information is vague, you can multiply the wrong look twenty times.

You do not need a new production stack to get started. You can organize a usable brand system in Canva, give ChatGPT the context to develop campaign ideas and copy, and give Claude Design the visual references to explore a direction. Then you can bring the strongest direction back into Canva to finish it. These tools do different jobs. They also need the same source of truth.

Here is how to set up that source, connect the tools where available, and use them on one real campaign.

## First, make a small brand pack

Collect the files and decisions you already trust. You need your correct logo files, approved colours with exact values, typefaces and font licences, a short description of the audience and offer, and three to five examples of work that feels unmistakably yours. Add examples of things you *do not* want, such as a particular stock-photo style or a phrase that sounds too salesy.

Write one page of rules: how the logo may sit on light and dark backgrounds, which colour is an accent rather than a field, how headlines and body copy differ, and what must be checked before anything goes live. If your brand has a product name or legal wording, write the exact form. Give this pack a date and an owner. An undated folder of old assets is an invitation to use the wrong version.

Do not treat an AI-generated brand description as the authority. The approved files and decisions are the authority. The tools should work from them.

## Set up Canva as the place where finished assets live

In Canva, go to **Brand**, choose or create the right **Brand Kit**, and add the approved logo versions, colours, fonts, and guidelines. Put a few approved layouts into Brand Templates if your plan supports them. A blank kit with a logo is not enough to teach anyone how your brand composes a page. One good social post, one presentation page and one ad layout give the team useful starting points. [Canva's Brand Kit guide](https://www.canva.com/help/brand-kit/) explains the current setup and team permissions.

Check the kit yourself. Open a blank design, apply a brand colour and font, and place the right logo on both a light and dark background. Make sure teammates can reach the correct kit, especially if your Canva account contains several brands. If your plan and role support Brand Controls, you can restrict colours, fonts or publishing. That is a team workflow choice, not a substitute for good design judgment.

Canva is where the campaign can be edited, resized and exported. It should also be where the final chosen version is reviewed at its real size. You can save an approved template for next time, but a template is a starting point. It does not decide whether this particular message and composition are right.

## Give ChatGPT the same rules for thinking and writing

Create a [ChatGPT Project](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/10169521-projects-in-chatgpt) for the brand or a defined campaign. Add the current brand pack as project files and put the fixed rules in Project instructions. Include the audience, offer, tone, terms to use, terms to avoid, and the approval rule: ChatGPT can draft concepts and copy, but a person checks facts, claims and final wording.

Now ask for *contrasting* campaign angles rather than twenty near-identical captions. A useful brief might say:

> We are launching an autumn coffee blend for local pickup. Our audience buys a bag for the hour between work and dinner. Use the attached brand pack. Give me three distinct campaign ideas, each with one promise, one headline, a short caption and a visual thought. Do not invent customer quotes, prices or product claims. Tell me where the brief is thin.

Select an angle because it fits the audience and product, not because the model called it the strongest. Edit the words until someone from your business would actually say them.

If you want to work with Canva from ChatGPT, [connect Canva in ChatGPT's Plugins settings](https://www.canva.com/help/connect-chatgpt-to-canva/) and authorize the correct Canva account. Turn Canva on in the relevant chat, ask it to create a specific design in Canva, preview the result, then open the chosen design in Canva to finish it. Canva says some Brand Template actions require a paid plan, and workspace admins may need to allow the connection. A connector can move a draft between tools. It does not guarantee that every draft obeys your brand rules.

## Give Claude Design visual evidence, not just adjectives

In [Claude Design](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/14604397-set-up-your-design-system-in-claude-design), create or select the correct organization and supply the brand assets: guidelines, logos, an existing deck, product examples or the design system code. Review the system Claude extracts. Check colours, fonts, layout patterns and components against the approved source. Fix any mismatch before publishing it for the team. Claude's documentation recommends testing the system on a real design before treating it as ready.

Use Claude Design to explore a campaign composition or one-pager from your chosen ChatGPT angle. Give it the actual headline and a short visual brief. Ask for two meaningfully different layouts, such as a typographic poster and a product-led composition, while respecting the same logo and palette rules. Review the result for hierarchy, crop, product truth and reading order. The word *on-brand* in a generated preview is not a quality check.

[Canva can also be connected to Claude](https://www.canva.com/help/connect-claude-to-canva/) through **Settings → Connectors** in Claude, then enabled for the chat. In supported Claude Design workflows, you can [send a direction to Canva](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/14604416-get-started-with-claude-design) for editable refinement. Availability and exact controls depend on your account and workspace. If that route is missing, export the approved direction and rebuild it against your Canva Brand Kit. The handoff matters more than the button used.

## Make one campaign with the three tools

Let's use the coffee example. The business wants people to buy an autumn blend for local pickup. The approved brand pack says cream packaging, deep brown type, a small copper accent, close-up product photography and plainspoken copy. There is no approved discount or claim about how the coffee will make someone feel.

**ChatGPT** develops three possible angles from that brief. One focuses on the ritual of coming home. Another focuses on the blend's actual tasting notes, if the product team has approved them. The third focuses on local pickup. The owner chooses local pickup because it gives the audience a concrete reason to act without inventing a benefit. The headline becomes: **Your evening starts here.** The caption gives the pickup details only after someone verifies them.

**Claude Design** tries two compositions with the same words and brand references. One is a large, spare headline beside the bag. The other uses a grid of product details. The owner chooses the first because it reads clearly on a phone and makes the product unmistakable. The second may be useful later as a product explainer, but it is not the launch ad.

**Canva** becomes the finishing table. Bring in the chosen direction, replace any generated pack image with the real product photo, use the Brand Kit's licensed font and correct logo, and build the final social post and static ad sizes. Check every crop, spelling, pickup fact and call to action. Save the approved layout as a reusable template. If you need variations, change one meaningful thing at a time, such as the headline or product framing, so you can tell what each version tests.

This is not a claim that the three-tool process matches Ads Studio's batch generation. Higgsfield's offer is fast static ad variation from stored brand and product data. This workflow is for people who want to establish the brand decisions, develop a campaign idea and control the final expression using tools they may already have.

## Keep the system agile without letting it drift

A living brand system needs a small update habit. Appoint one owner for the brand pack. When the offer, logo, type licence or audience changes, update the approved source first. Then update the Canva Brand Kit, ChatGPT Project files and instructions, and Claude Design system. Record the date and what changed. Remove obsolete examples rather than leaving them beside the new ones.

Once a month, run the same test brief through each tool. Ask for a headline, one layout and one final export. Check five things: correct logo, correct type and colour, right audience and offer, factual copy, and legibility at the size people will see. If one tool drifts, repair its context before producing another campaign. If all three drift, the source pack probably needs work.

The real advantage is not unlimited output. It is being able to move from an idea to a finished asset without explaining your brand from scratch each time. Set up the rules once, test them in each tool, and keep a person responsible for the choice that finally goes public.

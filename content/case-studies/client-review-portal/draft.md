# One link for the whole review

**Case study draft for The Dot. Client anonymous until written permission is on file.**

## The problem

For a client with a steady flow of social content, review had become a scattered task. The video sat in one place, the words to check in another, and the final decision came through a separate conversation. A video link that would not play could stop the whole review. It was too easy to wonder whether an edit had saved or whether a piece was ready.

I wanted the review to feel as clear as the finished content. That meant building around the way this client actually worked, including a phone-first reading experience, rather than asking her to adapt to another generic approval checklist.

## What I built

I made a private review page for each piece. The video plays on the page. Each on-screen frame sits beside the words that appear there, so the reviewer can see the moment and edit its copy in the same place. Covers and thumbnails have their own review spots.

Edits save while the reviewer types. On a phone, the editor opens full screen so a longer change is comfortable to make. After she sends feedback, the changed spot keeps her request visible while I apply it. The page shows the updated words and the piece's current state, so she can see what happened to the feedback.

On my side, a focused list shows what came back and where it belongs. The review page connects the creative work, the requested changes and the decision in one flow.

## Why this approach

A review system should make the work easier to judge. Putting the video, words and cover together removes the mental work of matching a comment to the right asset. Saving edits as they are made protects the review from a closed tab or a switch from phone to desktop. Keeping submitted feedback visible answers a simple, important question: *Did my change go through?*

This was built for a real production habit. The goal was a clear path from watching to editing to approval, with fewer loose ends for both sides.

## What changed

The client can now review the piece in one place, including its video, on-screen wording and visual assets. Requested edits stay attached to the exact spot they concern. Once the changes are applied, the page communicates the next state.

I am not attaching a time-saved number or a performance claim to this story. The work here is the experience itself: a calmer, more reliable way to make a decision on content before it is published.

## Closing line

If your team reviews content across a pile of links and email threads, the bottleneck may be the review experience. I design the system around the work people actually need to do.
